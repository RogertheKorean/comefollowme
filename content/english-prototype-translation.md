# [Proverbs 1–4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/1?lang=eng); [15–16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/15?lang=eng); [22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/22?lang=eng); [31](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/31?lang=eng); [Ecclesiastes 1–3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/eccl/1?lang=eng); [11–12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/eccl/11?lang=eng)

You might think of Proverbs as a book collecting the wise advice of loving parents (see [Proverbs 1:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/1?lang=eng&id=p8#p8)). Its central message is that life will go well when we seek wisdom, especially the wisdom of God. Yet the book that follows, Ecclesiastes, seems to say, “It is not quite that easy.” The Preacher in Ecclesiastes says that he devoted his heart to knowing wisdom, but found it like chasing the wind, with much sorrow ([Ecclesiastes 1:17–18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/eccl/1?lang=eng&id=p17-p18#p17)). In various ways the book asks: “Can there be lasting meaning in a world where everything seems empty, temporary, and uncertain?”

Although Proverbs and Ecclesiastes look at life from different perspectives, they teach similar truths. Ecclesiastes declares: “Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man” ([Ecclesiastes 12:13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/eccl/12?lang=eng&id=p13#p13)). A principle found throughout Proverbs is: “Trust in the Lord with all thine heart” ([Proverbs 3:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/3?lang=eng&id=p5#p5); see also [verse 7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/3?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)). When we trust and follow the Lord Jesus Christ, life may not always be perfect, but it is always better.

For an overview of Proverbs and Ecclesiastes, see “Proverbs” and “Ecclesiastes” in the Guide to the Scriptures.

## Ideas for Learning at Home and at Church

[**Proverbs 1–4**](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/1?lang=eng); [**15–16**](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/15?lang=eng); [**Ecclesiastes 1–3**](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/eccl/1?lang=eng); [**11–12**](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/eccl/11?lang=eng)

### “Incline thine ear unto wisdom”

Proverbs and Ecclesiastes are full of insights about wisdom. As you read [Proverbs 1–4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/1?lang=eng), [15–16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/15?lang=eng), [Ecclesiastes 1–3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/eccl/1?lang=eng), and [11–12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/eccl/11?lang=eng), mark the word “wisdom” and related words such as “knowledge” and “understanding.” What thoughts about wisdom come to you from these passages? Drawing on those thoughts, how would you describe the wisdom that the Lord gives ([Proverbs 2:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/2?lang=eng&id=p6#p6))? What blessings can God's wisdom bring us?

See also [Matthew 7:24–27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/7?lang=eng&id=p24-p27#p24) and [25:1–13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/25?lang=eng&id=p1-p13#p1).

**Help learners share what they learn.** “Give learners opportunities to talk with each other about what they are learning about the Savior and His gospel. These opportunities help them internalize and express the truths they have learned. They also help them gain confidence in their ability to share truth in other settings” (*Teaching in the Savior's Way*, [page 26](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/teaching-in-the-saviors-way-2022/07-part-2/11-invite-diligent-learning?lang=eng&id=p19#p19); this quotation is translated from the supplied Korean text). For example, invite learners to write down a reference in Proverbs or Ecclesiastes that gave them insight into God's wisdom, and then talk about what they learned.

### “Be not wise in thine own eyes: fear the Lord”

Another theme throughout Proverbs and Ecclesiastes is fearing the Lord. Watch for this expression as you read. What do you think it means to fear the Lord? Look for insights in Elder David A. Bednar's message [“Therefore They Hushed Their Fears”](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2015/04/therefore-they-hushed-their-fears?lang=eng) (*Liahona*, May 2015, pages 46–49). How is fearing the Lord different from other kinds of fear?

### “Trust in the Lord with all thine heart”

How could you persuade someone that trusting the Lord is better than relying on our own understanding ([Proverbs 3:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/3?lang=eng&id=p5#p5))? What analogy or object lesson would you use? As you ponder [Proverbs 3:5–7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/3?lang=eng&id=p5-p7#p5), consider how you would finish these sentences: *Trusting the Lord is like … . Relying on our own understanding is like … .*

Why is it unwise to rely on our own understanding? How have you come to know that the Lord can be trusted?

Even so, we may sometimes find it difficult to trust the Lord. Why might that be? In [“Trust Again”](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2021/10/51gong?lang=eng) (*Liahona*, November 2021, pages 97–99), Elder Gerrit W. Gong offers some reasons and helpful counsel. Which stories or teachings in this message could help someone regain trust in the Lord?

Both [Proverbs 3:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/3?lang=eng&id=p6#p6) and [Proverbs 4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/4?lang=eng) compare life to a path. What does it mean to let the Lord direct your paths ([Proverbs 3:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/3?lang=eng&id=p6#p6))? What in [chapter 4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/4?lang=eng) helps you consider the path your feet are taking (see the [footnote to verse 26](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/4?lang=eng&id=p26#p26))? For example, what do [verses 11–12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/4?lang=eng&id=p11-p12#p11) and [18–19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/4?lang=eng&id=p18-p19#p18) teach about the blessings of following the right path? What do [verses 26–27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/4?lang=eng&id=p26-p27#p26) mean to you?

See also [2 Nephi 31:18–21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/31?lang=eng&id=p18-p21#p18); “Lord, I Would Follow Thee,” *Hymns*, no. 220.

### “A soft answer turneth away wrath”

[Proverbs 15–16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/15?lang=eng) can inspire us to improve how we speak, especially to those we love. For example, think of a particular experience when a soft answer turned away anger ([Proverbs 15:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/15?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)).

You could also think of times when the Savior exemplified the teachings in [Proverbs 15:1–4, 18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/15?lang=eng&id=p1-p4,p18#p1) (see [Mark 12:13–17](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/mark/12?lang=eng&id=p13-p17#p13); [John 8:1–11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/8?lang=eng&id=p1-p11#p1)). How can you follow His example in your interactions with others?

How could the counsel in [Proverbs 15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/15?lang=eng) and [16:24–32](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/16?lang=eng&id=p24-p32#p24) apply to communication through today's digital devices? Look for verses that could serve as advice about communicating on social media or by text message.

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[**Proverbs 1:7**](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/1?lang=eng&id=p7#p7); [**2:5**](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/2?lang=eng&id=p5#p5); [**15:33**](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/15?lang=eng&id=p33#p33); [**16:6**](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/16?lang=eng&id=p6#p6); [**Ecclesiastes 12:13**](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/eccl/12?lang=eng&id=p13#p13)

### Fearing God means loving and obeying Him.

- To help children understand verses about fearing God, try replacing “fear” with words such as *reverence*, *love*, or *obedience* (see also [Hebrews 12:28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/12?lang=eng&id=p28#p28)). How does doing this change their understanding of the verses? How do we show that we fear the Lord?

[**Proverbs 3:5–7**](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/3?lang=eng&id=p5-p7#p5)

### I can trust the Lord with all my heart.

- With the children, make up actions that go with [Proverbs 3:5–7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/3?lang=eng&id=p5-p7#p5). Examples include making a heart with your hands, leaning to one side, walking in place, or pointing to your eyes. How can we show that we trust Jesus Christ with our whole heart?
- To illustrate what it means not to lean on your own understanding, invite the children to lean against something solid and stable, such as a wall ([Proverbs 3:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/3?lang=eng&id=p5#p5)). Then invite them to lean on something less stable, such as a broom. Or lean a stick or pencil against objects of differing firmness, such as a book or a sheet of paper. Why is it important to trust in the Lord rather than lean on our own understanding ([verse 5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/3?lang=eng&id=p5#p5))?

[Thoughtful woman and flowers](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/imgs/6afc477401a311efa291eeeeac1ea1493ae259da/full/%21500%2C/0/default)

*Learning to Trust the Lord*, Kathleen Peterson

[**Proverbs 15:1, 18**](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/15?lang=eng&id=p1,p18#p1)

### I can speak kindly.

- With the children, think of situations in which someone might become angry. Read [Proverbs 15:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/15?lang=eng&id=p1#p1) together and help them apply the verse to those situations. You could practice giving soft answers. A song about kindness, such as [“Love Is Spoken Here”](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/love-is-spoken-here?lang=eng) (*Children's Songbook*, pages 102–103), can reinforce this principle.
- To understand what it means to be slow to anger, share experiences when you or someone you know felt angry but chose to be kind ([Proverbs 15:18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/15?lang=eng&id=p18#p18)). Invite the children to think of things they can do *quickly* when they are slow to anger. For example, we can quickly think of Jesus, ask Heavenly Father for help, remember a Primary song, or leave the situation when possible.
